## 1. What the ICC is

The International Criminal Court (ICC) is a permanent international court
established to investigate and, where warranted, try individuals charged with the
gravest crimes of concern to the international community. It is based in **The
Hague, Netherlands**. It is an independent international organisation and is not
part of the United Nations, though it cooperates with the UN.

## 2. Founding instrument

The ICC was established by the **Rome Statute of the International Criminal
Court**, adopted in **1998**, which entered into force on **1 July 2002** after
the required number of States ratified it. States that join are known as **States
Parties** to the Rome Statute.

## 3. Jurisdiction — the core crimes

Under the Rome Statute, the Court may exercise jurisdiction over:

- **Genocide**
- **Crimes against humanity**
- **War crimes**
- **The crime of aggression**

The ICC is a court of **last resort**: under the principle of
**complementarity**, it acts only when national jurisdictions are unwilling or
genuinely unable to investigate or prosecute. Its jurisdiction is subject to the
temporal, territorial/nationality, and admissibility conditions set out in the
Rome Statute.

## 4. Principal organs

The Court is generally organised into:

- **The Presidency** — administration of the Court (other than the Office of the
  Prosecutor) and related functions.
- **The Judicial Divisions (Chambers)** — Pre-Trial, Trial, and Appeals
  Divisions, composed of the Court's judges.
- **The Office of the Prosecutor (OTP)** — independent organ responsible for
  examinations, investigations, and prosecutions.
- **The Registry** — non-judicial administration and servicing of the Court
  (support, operations, and public information).

## 5. How matters reach the Court

Situations may come before the Court by referral from a **State Party**, by
referral from the **United Nations Security Council**, or through the
**Prosecutor** initiating an investigation *proprio motu* (on their own
initiative) with Pre-Trial Chamber authorisation, in accordance with the Rome
Statute.
